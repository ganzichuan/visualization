# Visualization 2: Monthly Conditional Pass Rate

**Dataset:** City of Toronto DineSafe Open Data  
Official dataset page: <https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/dinesafe/>  
Reproducible CSV source used in the code: <https://github.com/benwebber/open-data-toronto-dinesafe/raw/refs/heads/main/data/dinesafe.2022.csv>

**Software used:** Excel-compatible spreadsheet workbook. I also exported a PNG version so the visualization can be viewed without opening the workbook.

**Intended audience:** Public-health managers, Toronto residents, and classmates who want to understand whether inspection outcomes changed across the year.

**Message:** This visualization focuses on the monthly share of inspections that resulted in a Conditional Pass in 2022. Most inspections resulted in Pass, so the rate rather than the raw count is more useful for comparing months. April has the highest Conditional Pass rate in this dataset, while several months remain below one percent.

**Design choices:** I used a monthly table with data bars because the values are small and would be easy to miss in a regular column chart. The data bars make relative differences visible, while the exact percentages remain available in the adjacent cells. The workbook also includes the raw monthly counts for Pass, Conditional Pass, Closed, and total inspections, so the reader can see how the rate was calculated.

**Reproducibility:** The underlying monthly summary is generated by the Python appendix from the same DineSafe CSV. The spreadsheet is less automatically reproducible than the Python figure because spreadsheet formatting can be changed manually. To reduce that problem, I included the source URL, the generated CSV summary, and the complete Python script that rebuilds the monthly data.

**Accessibility:** The visualization includes both data bars and numeric labels, so it does not depend on colour alone. The workbook has a clear title, readable headers, and a simple structure. The exported PNG also provides a static version for readers who cannot open Excel files.

**Impacted individuals and communities:** This visualization may shape how residents think about food safety and how businesses are perceived. Since Conditional Pass is a regulatory status rather than a moral judgment, I avoided identifying individual establishments and focused on citywide monthly patterns.

**Feature selection:** I used inspection ID, inspection date, and establishment status. I excluded establishment names, addresses, coordinates, and infraction details because the goal was to show temporal outcome patterns rather than evaluate specific businesses.

**Underwater labour:** The visualization required aggregating records to the inspection level, grouping inspections by month, calculating rates, checking that missing Closed values were handled as zero, formatting the workbook, and creating an accessible export.
